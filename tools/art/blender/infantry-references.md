# Infantry model references

Read when revising the flailman, crossbowman or pavise bearer.

Created 21 September 2026. These are original static low-poly models, generated by `infantry_batch.py` through the shared `build_assets.py` exporter. No game mesh, texture or screenshot is embedded in them. The existing miniature proportions, matte material response, faceted faces and +X forward convention are retained.

## References and choices

| Reference | Used for | Limits |
| --- | --- | --- |

Its ornate armoured character is not copied as the equipment of every ordinary crossbowman. |
| [The Met: A Deadly Art, European Crossbows](https://www.metmuseum.org/met-publications/a-deadly-art-european-crossbows-1250-1850) | Construction reference; the catalogue includes a Central European composite crossbow dated ca. 1425–75. Our model uses a dark composite bow, wooden tiller, pale lashing, nut, trigger lever, bolt case and simple belt hook. | Simplified mechanism and deliberately visible string/bolt thickness. Not a reconstruction of a particular surviving crossbow. |
| [Walters Art Museum: Archer's Shield, 51.1371](https://art.thewalters.org/object/51.1371/) | Large protective shield, raised centre, wood with surface covering, and the relationship to the bearer. | Museum dating is mid-15th century, after much of this campaign. The model uses a generic ribbed pavise with broad shoulders; no Teutonic insignia is copied. Width is exaggerated for readability. |
| [Charles University / ABCzech: Cep](https://www.abczech.cz/Cep-P7033642.html) | Long staff with an elongated wooden threshing head reinforced by iron bands and short studs. | Secondary overview, not measurements of a securely dated 1420 specimen. Our dimensions, hinge arrangement and carrying pose are illustrative. |

All three wear a simplified padded jack, coif under a low-crowned kettle hat, cloth hose and ankle shoes. The shared body (revised 23 September 2026) tapers the jack from rounded shoulders to the belt, adds rounded side-colour shoulders, and enlarges the head 8% about the neck. Forearms keep their pale padded defences, now with an elbow cop and quilted ridges; armoured variants render them as steel. Pole weapons are gripped below the chin so the face stays visible. These are readability choices for the battlefield camera, not new historical evidence. Shared clothing is a modest visual baseline for this batch, not a claim that the armies wore a uniform. More equipment, social-status and headgear variation can be added later.

## Assets and presentation

- `infantry_flail`: two-handed ready/carrying pose with the iron-bound striker hanging from a short joint; covers `CEPNICI` and `CEPNICI_PRASKY`.
- `infantry_crossbow`: crossbow held at chest height; covers `KUSINICI_HUSITI`, `KUSNICI`, `KUSNICI_JANOV` and `KUSINICI_PRASKY`. The Genoese type currently shares the same equipment, not a bespoke Genoese costume.
- `infantry_pavise`: tall painted shield with a central rib, wooden back and handle; sword visible above the far side. Covers `PAVEZNICI` and `PAVEZNICI_KRIZACI`. This is a carried ready pose; a planted/fortified variant remains future work.

`team_cloth` and `team_paint` are explicit colour slots. The renderer gives the new models muted red or blue accents according to their actual game side, while wood, iron, skin and neutral fabrics retain their colours. The shield has a plain pale central strip, with no faction-specific religious emblem. These are game readability colours, not researched historical uniforms. Original models without those slots are unchanged.

Rebuild only this batch from the repository root:

```sh
/opt/homebrew/bin/blender --background --factory-startup --python tools/art/blender/build_assets.py -- infantry_flail infantry_crossbow infantry_pavise
/opt/homebrew/bin/blender --background --factory-startup --python-exit-code 1 --python tools/art/blender/render_infantry_batch.py
python3 tools/art/blender/validate_exports.py
npm --prefix views/3d test
npm --prefix views/3d run build
```

The editable scenes are under `source/`, individual renders under `previews/`, and GLBs under `assets/3d/models/units/` at the repository root. The shared manifest records measured dimensions and triangle counts. Each export is static with no animation, rig, collider or LOD; named body and equipment parts remain separate in the Blender source for later pose variants.

## Spear and bow addition

The next pair is also generated by `infantry_batch.py` and retains the shared body proportions and material slots:

- `infantry_spear` covers `KOPINICI_HUSITI` and `KOPINICI`. The plain leaf-shaped steel head and socket are informed by [The Met's 15th-century Western European spear, 14.25.394](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/26796). That object is not identified as Bohemian. The upright pose and roughly 3.45 m overall model height are artistic choices; this is shorter than the 4–5 m spear described in the Hussite unit's lore, to suit the miniature scene and selection volume. It has no halberd blade or side hook.
- `infantry_archer` covers `LUCISTNICI`. A simple wooden bow is held drawn, with a nocked arrow, hip quiver and cloth cap. [This KCD II screenshot](https://static.deltiasgaming.com/2025/02/Kingdom-Come-Deliverance-II-Screenshot-2025.02.05-00.31.03.58-800x450.png), published in [Deltia's Gaming's bow guide](https://deltiasgaming.com/how-to-get-a-bow-in-kingdom-come-deliverance-2/), was inspected for the simple stave, grip and arrow position. [Royal Armouries' Hundred Years' War overview](https://royalarmouries.org/objects-and-stories/stories/the-hundred-years-war-1337-1453) supplies period context for longbows; it does not establish English archers in a particular Bohemian scenario. Our model is a generic foot archer, not a claim about national equipment or a Hungarian mounted/composite-bow specialist.

The spear and bow share the existing red/blue cloth mechanism. The archer definition remains on its original opposing side; the red preview is an available material variant, not a new roster entry. Existing polearm infantry, all statistics and scenario compositions are unchanged. More poses can be added later; these exports contain no animation.

Rebuild and render this pair:

```sh
/opt/homebrew/bin/blender --background --factory-startup --python-exit-code 1 --python tools/art/blender/build_assets.py -- infantry_spear infantry_archer
/opt/homebrew/bin/blender --background --factory-startup --python-exit-code 1 --python tools/art/blender/render_infantry_batch.py -- --spear-bow
```

The spear/bow addition passed all 61 3D tests, 20 shared presentation tests, the TypeScript/Vite build, and GLB/entrypoint checks. The exported models were inspected individually and together in red/blue; four five-figure formations were checked in the actual browser renderer with no warnings or errors. Archer selection and selection near a raised spear tip both worked. Geometry tests cover ground contact, low-poly budgets, static exports, and the complete selectable footprint and height on both sides. Broad rules/AI and native Bevy tests were not repeated for this presentation-only addition.
