# 3D models

`tools/art/blender/README.md` documents the kit and its rebuild commands. The `3d-diorama` skill covers the renderer, the browser loop and sharing the checkout. This skill covers how to change models safely.

## Art direction for models

- **Judge at game distance.** At full zoom a soldier is about 20 px tall. Silhouette, side colour and a readable head or hat matter; seams, rivets and quilting don't. Check a close-up and a far view.
- **Kingdom Come: Deliverance II is inspiration, not a spec.** It is set in 1403, about 20 years before these battles, so treat equipment as plausible, not as a uniform.
- **The pale forearms are padded arm armour** (`padded_linen`), not bare sleeves. Keep them. Armoured variants turn them into steel.
- **No cross motifs on Hussite gear.** A black cross reads as the crusaders' emblem. Wagon loopholes are plain slots for this reason. The chalice stays linen.
- **Side colour comes from material names.** The renderer recolours `team_cloth` and `team_paint` only. Other materials stay neutral.
- **Keep the faceted low-poly style.** Build from the kit primitives, rings and lofts; high-subdivision spheres look lumpy next to everything else.
- **Show before/after renders before committing.** Taste-sensitive changes need the user's eye.

## Which code feeds which models

- **`InfantryBatch.body()`** (`infantry_batch.py`) is the shared figure: spear, archer, flail, crossbow, pavise, polearm, halberd, handgun, shield, gunner, the three cavalry riders, commanders, the dismounted knight, the blockhouse garrison and the wagon crew. `padded=False` with a `coat` material gives the unarmoured civilians.
- **Part names are hooks.** The armoured noble and knights (`people_batch.foot_body`, `cavalry_batch.heavy_armour`) turn every `<prefix>_lower_sleeve*` into steel and `_upper_sleeve*` or `_coif` into mail. Name new arm parts to match. Bascinets, visors and breastplates use hard-coded coordinates, so re-render them after changing the head or torso.
- **`Fortifications.gable_roof()`** is shared by the houses, barn, well, monastery wing, gatehouse and manor.
- **`horse()`** (`build_assets.py`) is the animated `cavalry.glb` and, in a standing pose, every `cavalry_*` model. Keep the `Fore_*`/`Hind_*` hip and `Knee` empties and the tail's pivot for `HorseWalk`, the saddle height riders sit on, and the length. The heavy horse's forehead guard follows the head line.
- **Settlement buildings** are in `settlement_batch.py`, including the village `church`. **Units** are in `support_units.py`, including `war_wagon`.
- **The old kit `soldier()` and `wagon()`** now feed only the animated `cavalry.glb` rider and the unarmed-adult experiment export. Don't build new models on them. `build_battle_assets.py` flattens the live `war_wagon` and removes its crew by name prefix (`Wagon_crew_`): keep that prefix if you rename wagon parts.

## Limits the renderer relies on

These fail tests, or silently change gameplay presentation:

- **Formation envelope** (`views/3d/src/formation-envelope.ts`, test `formation-envelope.test.ts`). Every unit vertex must lie inside one convex outline, shared by all units and rotated through every facing. The tallest unit must reach 4.1265 m after recipe scale, and nothing may pass 4.13 m. That maximum is the war wagon's banner top at 3.94 m, times its 1.05 recipe scale. Town-wall gates and routes are sized from this envelope. Keep a remodelled unit inside its old plan outline and height, and check with `check_outline.py`. It is conservative: other units' outlines give some slack, so the test remains the real gate.
- **Settlement footprints.** `views/3d/src/settlement-models.ts` must equal the manifest's width and depth to within 1 mm, and the GLB's height to within 3 mm. The kit `church`'s recorded size is inflated by an unapplied spire rotation; `Settlement.fit()` maps a rebuilt model onto an exact envelope. Changing `settlement-models.ts` instead means rebuilding the renderer bundle (see "Sharing the checkout" in `3d-diorama`).
- **Triangle budgets** (asset tests): infantry figures at most 2,500, each mount under 4,000, environment models under 10,000. The war wagon, with three crew, is about 11,000 and has no test budget. Mind it anyway: armies field many wagons.
- **Formation offsets and pick radii** in `unit-recipes.ts` assume the current model sizes.

## Build only what changed

- **Blender needs native graphics.** Run it outside the command sandbox; inside it, the Metal probe crashes before Python starts.
- **Build by name:** `blender --background --factory-startup --python-exit-code 1 --python tools/art/blender/build_assets.py -- <names>`. List every model that uses the code you changed; the map above tells you which.
- **A whole-kit rebuild rewrites every GLB, preview and `.blend` byte-wise,** even when nothing changed. If you do one, compare the manifest with `HEAD` to find the entries whose triangles or dimensions changed. Restore everything else with `git checkout`.
- **Delete the untracked `.blend1` backups** the build writes under `tools/art/blender/source/`, and restore any tracked ones it touched.
- **zsh doesn't word-split `$VAR`.** Pass Blender arguments literally or as an array, not as `$ARGS`.

## Verify and show

1. Before editing, copy `assets/3d/models` to your scratchpad as the "before" set.
2. Rebuild, then run `python3 tools/art/blender/validate_exports.py` and `npm --prefix views/3d test`.
3. For units, run `python3 tools/art/blender/check_outline.py <before.glb> <after.glb>`.
4. Render both sets with the same camera: `render_compare.py -- --root <before dir> --output before.png <models>`, then again with no `--root`. `--red` applies side colours and `--yaw` turns the view.
5. Refresh the committed galleries you affected: `render_infantry_batch.py` (with `-- --spear-bow` or `-- --cavalry`), `render_unit_completion.py`, `render_settlement_batch.py`, `render_fortification_batch.py`, `render_artillery_batch.py`, `render_camp_batch.py`, `render_landscape_batch.py`.
6. Look in the game (browser loop in `3d-diorama`). The `+` zoom button gets closest. Quick Battle replaces the resume slot's autosave, so start a named battle with `?dev=1` instead.
7. Update the docs: the README asset table, the matching `*-references.md` (dimension and triangle tables; the radius column is the largest horizontal distance from the origin) and `docs/MODEL_INVENTORY.md`.
8. Commit only your files, staged by path (another session may share the checkout). Send the user the before/after image.
